The Giant Steps Generator

Four-Note Pitch Cells and Rhythmic Permutations

The Giant Steps Generator, based on John Coltrane's composition Giant Steps, is an interactive jazz improvisation tool that generates melodic variations over Coltrane's rapidly changing harmonic progression using four-note pitch cells.

Rather than relying on pentatonic scales, the application uses a specific four-note formula:

1 – 2 – 3 – 5

Each chord is assigned four pitches derived from its root and chord quality. These pitches form the exclusive melodic vocabulary available over that harmony.

For example, Bmaj7 produces:

B – C♯ – D♯ – F♯

The generator offers five ways of organizing these four pitches:

Melodic PatternScale-Degree Order
Straight1 – 2 – 3 – 5
Retrograde5 – 3 – 2 – 1
Inverted Pair1 – 3 – 2 – 5
Cry Me a River5 – 1 – 2 – 3
Enclosure2 – 1 – 3 – 5

These patterns reorganize the same four pitches rather than introducing additional notes.

Coltrane's harmonic framework, built around major tonal centers separated by major thirds, presents a particular challenge for melodic continuity. The generator explores how restricted four-note collections can navigate these rapid harmonic movements while maintaining coherent melodic lines.

The application follows the original 16-measure harmonic progression, including measures containing two chords. Each chord change introduces its corresponding four-note collection.

Rhythmic variation provides an additional dimension of melodic development. The generator combines prescribed rhythmic structures with the selected pitch-cell orderings, producing new melodic exercises while maintaining harmonic and rhythmic constraints.

Four melodic modes, Angular, Smooth, Triadic, and Quartal, offer different approaches to organizing the selected pitches across registers and melodic intervals.

The result is controlled musical randomness, producing a virtually unlimited variety of melodic exercises within clearly defined harmonic, rhythmic, and intervallic boundaries.

The application is not intended to replace the combination of pencil, manuscript paper, and your ears traditionally associated with developing improvisational vocabulary. Rather, it offers a complementary way to hear the musical possibilities created by these constraints, develop fluency with four-note melodic cells under your fingers, navigate Coltrane's challenging harmonic movement, and strengthen your sight-reading skills through continually changing melodic material.
